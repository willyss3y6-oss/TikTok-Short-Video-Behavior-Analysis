# TikTok Short-Video Behavior Analysis

TikTok's Addictive Algorithm: Its Effects on Short-Term Memory

This project started as a research  collage project about the effects of TikTok and short-form videos on memory, attention, and daily behavior.

I created the survey myself and collected responses from 40 participants.
I then organized the results and used statistical charts to explore patterns in TikTok usage and how participants feel it affects their memory 
attention span, productivity, and behavior.

Project Questions

The main questions I wanted to explore were:

* Does TikTok use affect short-term memory?
* Is there a relationship between frequent TikTok use and attention span?
* Can repeated content influence people's beliefs or worldview?
* Are there behaviors that may be related to addictive short-video use?

 Data Collection

The data was collected through an online survey.

* Number of participants: 40
* Data collection method: Online survey using Google Forms
* Data type: Self-reported responses

The survey included questions about TikTok usage, memory, attention, productivity, mood, scrolling behavior, and the influence of repeated content.

 Some Findings

Here are some of the results from the survey:

* 92.5% of participants had been using TikTok for more than one year.
* 52.5% reported spending 19–20 hours per week on TikTok.
* 50% said they had tried to delete TikTok but returned to using it.
* 87.5% said the short length of videos makes it easy to keep watching.

 Memory

* 17.5% remembered the last three videos they watched.
* 5% remembered the last ten videos they watched.
* 35% reported difficulty remembering details from lectures, books, or conversations.
* 47.5% noticed changes in their memory after stopping TikTok use.

Attention and Behavior

* 20% felt that their attention span had shortened.
* 25% found it harder to watch long lectures or movies.
* 22.5% felt that short videos made their thinking more fragmented.
* 20% reported lower tolerance for boredom.

 Influence of Content

* 37.5% said repeated content had influenced their personal beliefs.
* 27.5% said repeated exposure to certain ideas or lifestyles had influenced their worldview.
* 55% knew someone who had been affected or scammed by false medical information on TikTok.

Limitations

This project has some limitations.

The sample size is relatively small with 40 participants and the data is based on self-reported responses. The survey also does not include a control group.

Because of this the results should be interpreted as patterns and participant perceptions rather than proof that TikTok directly causes these effects.
